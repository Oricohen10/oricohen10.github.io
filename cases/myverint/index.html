Verint's workforce ran on two mobile apps - one for agents, one for supervisors - both inherited from desktop and never designed for the floor. A new AI mandate gave us the reason to rebuild from scratch.

Background

Verint ran two separate mobile apps for years - one for supervisors, one for agents. Both grew out of the desktop WFE product as afterthoughts. Mobile was a free add-on: no dedicated budget, no design owner, no system. The work passed hand to hand, accumulating inconsistencies and accessibility gaps along the way.

The catalyst arrived in 2024: a mandate to integrate Ask Verint - Verint's AI agent - into the mobile experience. You can't build something new on a broken foundation, so we rebuilt the foundation first.

Challenge

The problems ran deep: no design system, no accessibility baseline, no documentation, and no access to half the existing flows. Parts of the app were invisible to design entirely.

The harder challenge was organizational. Stakeholders wanted every desktop capability on mobile - one to one. I disagreed. Desktop and mobile serve different use cases. A supervisor plans next month's schedule from a desktop. A contact center agent adding 15 minutes to their shift does it from their phone, between calls, on the floor.

Porting desktop features into small screen real estate - without asking if they belonged there - was the wrong brief. Rewriting it took time and more than logic to move.

Solution

We merged both apps into one, with role-based capability views - supervisors and agents each see what they need, not everything at once.

Alongside that, we built My Verint's first mobile design system: color tokens, type scales, responsive behaviors, motion specs, and documentation. A shared language the team had never had.

The biggest outcome: Ask Verint users now complete all their tasks through the AI tab - in natural language, without navigating a single screen. The design work made that possible. The data validated every mobile-first call we made.

The Design

MyVerint organizes every workflow into five purpose-built tabs. Agents, managers, and back-office teams each get a view designed around their role, with an AI assistant that ties it all together.

Result

My Verint replaced both legacy apps - one unified experience across iOS and Android. The mobile design system gave every product team a foundation to build on that hadn't existed before.

The number that tells the whole story: 85% of users complete all their tasks through Ask Verint, without touching a single screen. A contact center agent can check their schedule, request time off, and review KPIs in a single conversation. The product changed not just how the app looks, but how people work.

What I'd do differently
Process

Bring data to stakeholder conversations before the wireframes.

I spent weeks arguing mobile-first scope on instinct and logic. A few user interviews and usage analytics - showing where and how people actually used the app - would have replaced those debates with a single slide and moved the project faster.

Research

Involve real contact center agents earlier.

Our testing happened too late, which meant assumptions got baked in before we could validate them. Earlier access to actual users - not internal proxies - would have let us test the core premise before committing to the design direction.
